Why the helmet and seatbelt matter

42%

lower risk of death for riders who wear a helmet correctly. It cuts head injuries by 69%.

45–50%

fewer deaths among drivers and front-seat passengers wearing a seatbelt. Rear-seat belts cut deaths and serious injuries by up to 25%.

50,029

people died in India in 2022 in crashes while not wearing a helmet. 16,715 died not wearing a seatbelt.

Sources: WHO road traffic injuries fact sheet; MoRTH, Road Accidents in India 2022.

Myths vs facts

"It's a short trip, I don't need a helmet."

Most crashes happen close to home, at everyday speeds.

"Back-seat passengers don't need belts."

An unbelted rear passenger is thrown forward with many times their body weight and can injure the people in front.

"A loose helmet is good enough."

An unstrapped helmet often comes off before your head hits the road. Always click the chin strap.

At a crash scene: the golden hour

The first 60 minutes after a crash decide survival. Stay calm and follow these steps.

  1. Switch on hazard lights and make the spot safe. Stand away from traffic.
  2. Call 112 or 108. Give the exact spot: road name, landmark, direction.
  3. Don't remove a rider's helmet unless they can't breathe.
  4. Don't move the injured person unless there is fire or other danger.
  5. Press firmly on bleeding with a clean cloth.
  6. If unconscious but breathing, keep the airway open and stay with them.

Good Samaritan law (Sec 134A, MV Act): people who help crash victims cannot be held liable or forced to give their details.
